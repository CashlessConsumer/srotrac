SROTrac Weekly #5: UFF's first roster exit; FACE's board and its big-lender report

Week to 5 October 2026

TL;DR

  • UFF lost a member. BSFG Finance, listed as a full member since our captures began, vanished from UFF's published member wall between our 30 September and 1 October fetches — taking the roster from 121 to 120 and shaving the register's total to 773 listed memberships. It is the first UFF roster movement since its 10 September recognition, and neither UFF nor BSFG has said why. [1]
  • FACE refreshed its board. PhonePe Lending CEO Hemant Gala and Navi MD & CEO Rajiv Naresh were elected Member Directors; Avtar Monga and Srinath Sridharan moved to Independent Director; Ashish Goyal left the board but stays on the Audit, Finance & Remuneration Committee. [2] The same SRO-FT then published a Digital Personal Loans report showing digital NBFCs now originate 70% of personal-loan sanction volume. [3]
  • IRDAI's distribution reset is "not final". Chairman Ajay Seth says every comment with a rationale will be considered, the expense cuts will arrive as a phased glide path, and draft regulations will face a second public consultation — with comments on the current paper open till 25 October. [4] Still no public statement from the Life or General Insurance Councils, the statutory SROs of the industry being reset. [5]

Roster & register changes

UFF: BSFG Finance out, 121 → 120 — first movement since recognition. Our daily member-wall capture of unifiedfintech.in still listed BSFG Finance (a member-tier row in our data) on 30 September 2026; the 1 October capture no longer did, and four consecutive daily fetches (1–5 October) confirm the removal. [1] UFF's own site was modified on 30 September (per the page's schema metadata), and its About page now states flatly: "At present UFF has 120 members." [6] No announcement from UFF, and no statement from BSFG, explains the exit; we looked and found none.

Two things worth saying plainly. First, the exit is visible only because UFF publishes its roster — this is the transparency dividend working, and the reason the no-public-roster club (eight of the register's 18 bodies) matters. Second, visibility without a stated reason is half the protection: a consumer cannot tell whether BSFG left, was asked to leave, or was dropped. SROs that enforce conduct codes should say so when membership ends — the enforcement story is the story.

The register's totals moved for the first time since the expansion: 18 SROs / 4 regulators / 773 listed member orgs / 79 in 2+ SROs / 2 SRO-FTs. [1] The overlap count held at 79 because BSFG sat only in UFF.

FACE board refresh, now on the record. FACE's governance page now lists Hemant Gala (PhonePe) and Rajiv Naresh (Navi) as Member Directors, alongside a board where Avtar Monga and Srinath Sridharan are Independent Directors and Ashish Goyal no longer sits as a member — he remains on the Audit, Finance & Remuneration Committee. [7] Our leadership.csv and the FACE register page mirror the refreshed board as verified on 2 October 2026. The trade press reported the elections in late September. [2]

Read together with the report below, the composition point is hard to miss: the two biggest consumer-facing digital-lending platforms in the country now hold Member Director seats at the SRO-FT that writes the conduct codes they lend under. That is not an accusation — board seats for large members are how RBI's SRO-FT framework is designed — but it is exactly where a consumer-facing register should be keeping score.

The SROs at work

FACE's Digital Personal Loans report (1 October) is the week's most substantial SRO output. Built on CRIF High Mark bureau data covering 110+ digital NBFCs from April 2022 to June 2026, it puts the scale of the SRO-FT's constituency in numbers: in Q1 FY26-27 digital NBFCs sanctioned 3.4 crore loans worth ₹64,656 crore, with an average ticket of ₹18,802 — against ₹70,025 for other NBFCs and ₹4.52 lakh for banks. Digital loans were 22% of total personal-loan sanction value but 70% of sanction volume. Growth was fastest outside the metros' usual map: among the top 10 states, Andhra Pradesh led (+38% sanction value year-on-year), then Uttar Pradesh (+35%) and Tamil Nadu (+34%), with Delhi slowest (+26%). [3] [8]

Why a consumer desk reads it closely: this is the SRO describing its own beat — small-ticket, high-volume, unsecured credit to households. The same report's existence argues for the SRO's consumer work to keep pace with the volume: a 70% volume share means most new borrowers in the country are meeting regulated credit for the first time through FACE's members.

FIDC held a member webinar on regulatory requirements and conduct (1 October) — pitched at "strengthening responsible, transparent and sustainable business practices across the NBFC sector". [9] Modest, but it is the NBFC SRO doing visible member-education work; we will watch whether materials are published.

IRDAI follow-up: the chairman softens the landing. In interviews after the 23 September distribution paper, Ajay Seth said the proposals "are not final", that feedback "backed by sound economic or business logic" will be considered, that expense limits will come down via a phased glide path rather than overnight, and that draft regulations will be re-published for a second consultation before any final notification. [4] He also said Bima Sugam should be operational in four to six months. [10] Insurers had already met him on 28 September seeking exactly that: a softer glide path on commissions and differentiated expense limits. [11]

Where are the councils? For a second straight week, we found no public statement from the Life Insurance Council or the General Insurance Council on the distribution consultation — the reset lands squarely on the two statutory bodies' territory (commissions, expenses, distributor conduct). Comments close 25 October; if either council files, its submission should be public. [5]

Everyone else was quiet. No announcements found in the week's coverage from FIMMDA, FEDAI, MFIN, Sahamati, SRPA, Sa-Dhan, BASL, AIBI, AMFI or ANMI. A git diff of every member CSV since Weekly #4 shows exactly one roster change: UFF's. [1]

What it means for consumers

  • If UFF's roster can shrink silently, ask why when any SRO's changes. A member leaving a conduct-policing body is material to borrowers who rely on that badge. UFF's published list made the exit visible; the missing piece is the stated reason. [1]
  • Your lender is now likely a digital NBFC. 70% of personal-loan volume flows through the constituency FACE supervises, at an average ticket of ₹18,802. Before borrowing, check the lender's RBI registration and the app in FACE's DLA validator; small-ticket does not mean small consequences. [3] [8]
  • The PhonePe–Navi seats at FACE are now on the register. Watch how the SRO-FT's standards work treats lending practices of its largest board members — that is the test of whether the framework's governance provisions bite. [2] [7]
  • Three weeks left to comment on insurance distribution economics. Seth has committed to considering "each comment with a rationale" before draft regulations. If commissions and embedded sales costs matter to your premiums, this is the window. [4] [10]
