SROTrac Weekly #11: 450 claimed, 86 on the wall; RBI draws the SROs' lines at MFIN's own event; the 50% cap holds; eight days to the AA meter

Week to 23 November 2026

TL;DR

  • 450 claimed, 86 on the wall. FACE's official LinkedIn page posted this week (16 November, marked edited): "With 450+ members and growing, the Fintech Association for Consumer Empowerment (FACE) is India's largest FinTech association and RBI-recognised Self-Regulatory Organisation in the FinTech sector (SRO-FT)" — alongside a recruitment hook: "for a limited time, your first year of FACE membership is complimentary." [1] FACE's own membership page, captured daily, lists 86 organisations. [2] The marketing number is more than five times the published wall — and with it, both of RBI's SRO-FTs now advertise member counts their own properties don't reconcile: UFF says "200+ member institutions" in its newsletter while its About page says 120 beside a wall missing BSFG Finance, now day 53. [3] [4] [5]
  • RBI drew the SROs' lines at MFIN's own event. At the 13 November Mumbai launch of MFIN's "Micro Matters, Macro View" (6th edition), Deputy Governor Shirish Chandra Murmu — in a transcript published by BIS this week (19 November) — told the microfinance SRO directly: "Guidelines issued by SROs without prior regulatory approval may inadvertently venture into areas of regulatory ambiguity or diverge from the broader regulatory intent... SROs should refrain from what could be construed as interpretations or clarifications of existing regulatory provisions." [6] A regulator saying this at an SRO's flagship event is the clearest statement yet of where the self-regulatory layer's pen stops.
  • The 50% cap holds. Murmu rejected the industry's ask to exclude principal repayments from the FOIR calculation: excluding loans "would defeat this purpose, tinker with the sanctity of the limit and understate household leverage. Let us also remember that 50 per cent is already a liberal threshold." Evidence cited: an NCAER study (March 2026) finds the average microfinance borrower spends just 18.7% of monthly household income on repayment, with close to two-thirds going to consumption — so the FOIR "should be treated as a prudential maximum – not a target that every household has to meet." [6]
  • Conditional openness on the income threshold. On the ₹3 lakh annual household-income eligibility line, Murmu said "a review will be undertaken depending on our assessment of the need for such a revision and on developments in the industry" — with a warning that industry must first "fortify its household income assessment and reporting processes." [6]
  • RBI meets the NBFC layer today. On 23 November, Governor Sanjay Malhotra and all deputy governors meet senior NBFC executives — housing finance companies and microfinance institutions included — with microfinance stress, gold loans and cybersecurity on the agenda. [7]
  • The regulator-side calendar has two December dates now. IRDAI's 10 November office order (IRDAI/RBC/ORD/MISC/150/11/2026) constituted two risk-based-capital working groups — life (Jamuna Choudhary convening) and non-life (Parmeshwar Shelke) — with one month to produce an exposure draft of RBC regulations and an approach paper: due around 10 December. [8] [9] That lands days before the revised distribution draft expected mid-December. [10]
  • Sahamati keeps publishing; the meter still has no price card. The SRO rolled out SamvAAd 2026 case-study posts through the week — Finlead AI on insurance underwriting (17 November), FinAGG on MSME credit (18 November), InPrime on informal-economy lending and Finarkein on new-to-credit borrowers (18 November). [11] Eight days from now (1 December) the AA ecosystem's first standard fee model switches on; the fee amounts have still not appeared in any public document. [10] [12]
  • The walls held. No member wall moved this week in our daily captures. The register holds at 764 listed memberships across 18 SROs / 4 regulators / 79 orgs in 2+ SROs / 2 SRO-FTs, and all 23 tracked official accounts remain linked from their SROs' own sites (checked 23 November). [2] [13]

Roster & register changes

A quiet week on the walls. Our daily captures found no additions or exits anywhere on the register this week: FACE 86, UFF 120, MFIN 74, FEDAI 108, Sahamati 110, FIMMDA 116 (the 8 May 2025 PDF roster), SRPA 18, AMFI 56, and the councils at 49 and 27. [2]

The number above the door, now at both SRO-FTs. FACE's 16 November LinkedIn post claims "450+ members and growing" and offers a complimentary first year to new joiners. [1] Its published wall: 86 organisations. [2] UFF's pattern is the same shape: "200+ member institutions" in its Q2 newsletter and conclave material, "At present UFF has 120 members" on its About page (re-fetched today), and a wall that has omitted BSFG Finance since 1 October with no public statement from either party — day 53. [3] [4] [5] Neither body publishes a dated register with additions and exits, so neither number can be checked against anything except the walls we capture. Until one of them does, every advertised count stays a claim — and the captures stay the only public record of the movement.

The drift check holds. All 23 official accounts on the social register remain linked from their SROs' own websites (checked 23 November). [13]

The SROs at work

MFIN: the venue for the sector's most consequential speech this quarter. The 6th edition of MFIN's flagship publication "Micro Matters, Macro View" launched 13 November in Mumbai with RBI Deputy Governor Shirish Chandra Murmu as the speaker; the full transcript went up on BIS on 19 November. [6] The sector, per the speech, is "moving from cautious consolidation towards a modest recovery" — and the regulator used the platform to set four markers:

One, the FOIR cap stays at 50% and stays whole. The industry had asked for principal repayments to be excluded from the calculation on certain loans; the Deputy Governor's refusal rests on arithmetic the regulator clearly wants on the record: the NCAER study (March 2026) shows the average borrower spending 18.7% of monthly household income on repayment, NABARD's rural surveys show consumption absorbing roughly three-fourths of income, and 50% "is already a liberal threshold" — a prudential maximum, not a target. [6]

Two, the ₹3 lakh income threshold is under conditional review — review "depending on our assessment of the need," conditioned on the industry fixing income assessment and reporting first. [6]

Three, the conduct warnings are getting more specific: lending rates rising "across all categories of regulated entities," average loan sizes climbing (with the pointed question of whether larger loans service older dues), income overstatement risk, and recovery conduct — with the Key Facts Statement to be explained "in a language the borrower understands," not just signed. [6] [14]

Four, the one this register exists for. Murmu, addressing the microfinance SRO's own event: "Guidelines issued by SROs without prior regulatory approval may inadvertently venture into areas of regulatory ambiguity or diverge from the broader regulatory intent. So, while building consensus towards disciplined lending, SROs should refrain from what could be construed as interpretations or clarifications of existing regulatory provisions." [6] Read plainly: the SRO's lane is consensus-building, monitoring and escalation to RBI — not writing quasi-regulation. For a register that tracks what self-regulatory bodies actually publish, this is the boundary line stated by the regulator itself, and it applies with equal force across the 18 bodies we track.

Today: RBI sits down with the NBFC layer. Governor Malhotra and all deputy governors meet NBFC leadership — housing finance and microfinance executives included — this evening; microfinance stress, gold loans and cybersecurity are the reported agenda. [7] After a speech that flagged pricing and loan sizes, the meeting is where industry pushes back — or doesn't.

IRDAI: the RBC clock started 10 November. Office order IRDAI/RBC/ORD/MISC/150/11/2026 constituted two working groups — life insurers (convened by Jamuna Choudhary) and non-life (Parmeshwar Shelke) — to build the risk-based capital framework on Ind AS statements, with an exposure draft of the RBC regulations and an approach paper due within a month. [8] [9] The life group draws CFOs and appointed actuaries from LIC, SBI Life, HDFC Life, ICICI Pru Life, Bajaj Life, Tata AIA, Kotak Life, Bandhan Life and Aditya Birla Life plus Munich Re and the Institute of Actuaries of India; the non-life group mirrors it with New India, ICICI Lombard, Bajaj General, SBI General, Tata AIG, Niva Bupa, IndusInd General and Kiwi General plus Swiss Re. [9] A mid-December RBC exposure draft plus the mid-December revised distribution draft means the insurance layer's December will be heavy — and both drafts will pass through consultation windows where BASL-regulated advisers, brokers and councils will want seats. [10]

Sahamati: the SamvAAd afterglow continues. Four case-study posts went up through the week: Finlead AI on using AA data and AI agents to compress insurance underwriting (17 November), FinAGG on GST-plus-AA data for MSME credit (18 November), and InPrime and Finarkein on informal-economy and new-to-credit lending (18 November). [11] Useful ecosystem evidence — and note the pattern: the SRO publishes member use-cases at volume while the fee model its own chair lists as priority one ("building a sustainable economic model") has eight days left before switch-on and still no published fee card. [10] [12]

What it means for consumers

  • The 50% FOIR cap is your floor, and RBI just defended it. Your total monthly loan repayments across regulated lenders should never be pushed beyond half your household income — and the regulator rejected making that line easier for lenders to cross. [6] The flip side, straight from the Deputy Governor: the cap is a maximum, not a target. If a lender's product design walks you up to 50%, that lender is flagging itself. [6]
  • The KFS is not a formality. RBI's requirement is that the Key Facts Statement be explained — charges included — in a language you understand, before you sign. "Understanding" is the standard, not signature. If the explanation doesn't happen, that is itself the violation to cite in your complaint. [6] [14]
  • Don't take advertised member counts as endorsements. FACE says 450+; its wall shows 86. UFF says 200+; its About page says 120 and its wall has an unexplained hole. [1] [2] [3] [4] When a lender's pitch leans on "member of India's largest SRO," the only checkable record is the published wall — and even that, per UFF's BSFG gap, moves without explanation. Ask which members, check the wall.
  • Eight days to the AA meter. From 1 December the AA ecosystem's standard fee model switches on. The consumer guarantee holds: you cannot be charged for sharing your own financial data through the network. What to watch: any lender adding a new "statement fee," "data fee" or "processing fee" after 1 December — ask whether it is the AA pass-through in disguise, since the actual fee amounts have never been published. [10] [12]
  • If you're a microfinance borrower with a recovery problem: the three conduct concerns RBI named — over-indebtedness, high interest rates, harsh recovery — are now on the record at the regulator's own deputy-governor level. [6] The escalation path is unchanged: the lender's grievance desk first, then RBI Ombudsman; MFIN is a lever, not a court. [14]
  • December is the consumer's month in insurance. Two drafts are due within days of each other — RBC (~10 December) and distribution (mid-December). [8] [10] The distribution draft is the one with your premium in it: if the commission compression it proposes doesn't visibly reach borrower pricing, it has re-balanced industry margins and called it consumer protection.
