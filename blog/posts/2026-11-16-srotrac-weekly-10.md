# SROTrac Weekly #10

_Week to 16 November 2026_

## TL;DR

- **The feedback mountain has a number now.** IRDAI has received over two lakh comments and suggestions on its proposed distribution reforms from around 15,000 stakeholders — insurers, agents, brokers and policyholders among them — sources told ET Now on 13 November. Draft regulations are likely by mid-December 2026, with the final reforms expected to take effect from 1 April 2027. [1] That is up from the 650+ submissions on record when the window closed on 31 October, and the working start date has quietly consolidated onto April — the January option has faded from this week's reporting. [2]
- **The brokers went to Delhi and were sent back.** On 10 November, insurance brokers met Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman, PMO officials and the Department of Financial Services seeking government intervention against the proposed caps. They were directed to present their objections to IRDAI chairman Ajay Seth. The Insurance Brokers Association of India opposes hard caps, wants the 2023 expense-of-management framework to continue until its scheduled 2028 review, and seeks an independent impact assessment. [3] CNBC-TV18 reported the same day that IRDAI is considering modifications to the draft paper based on the feedback — PB Fintech's shares rose nearly 5% on the news. [4]
- **Sahamati counts its next chapter — with a date stuck in the past.** The SRO's blog post on CEO B.G. Mahesh's SamvAAd welcome address, published 12 November with the video on 11 November, lists the network at over 500 million consents fulfilled, 350 million accounts linked and nearly 1,200 entities live as of September 2026, with AA-enabled lending running at about ₹35,000 crore in monthly disbursements in H2 FY26 and a stated ambition to grow that fivefold in two years. [5] [6] But the same post describes the ecosystem's fee model as "effective from 1 November 2026" — eleven days after Sahamati's own circular moved the switch-on to 1 December. [5] [7] The fee amounts remain unpublished; "subsequent circulars" are still promised. [7] [2]
- **UFF's merger math doesn't reach its own wall.** UFF's Q2 FY27 newsletter confirms the merger with the India Fintech Foundation — the 12 August Conclave was "the first edition since the merger... into a single industry body, now uniting 200+ member institutions under one voice," and the 46th UFF board meeting on 23 September 2026 was held "to finalise on Merger Options of UFF-IFF." [8] UFF's About page, re-fetched today, still reads "At present UFF has 120 members," and its member wall still omits BSFG Finance — day 46 of the silent exit. [9] [10] [11]
- **FACE in the collection economy.** A Business Standard feature (15 November) on how technology and consent norms are reshaping debt collection leans on FACE's debt-recovery guidelines — released September 2025 — and quotes CEO Sugandh Saxena on regulation defining "no-go zones" for collections. [12] And an ETBFSI explainer (10 November) walks through what the two SRO-FTs can do, where their authority ends, and the challenges facing the framework. [13]
- **The walls held.** No member wall moved this week in our daily captures. The register holds at **764** listed memberships across 18 SROs / 4 regulators / 79 orgs in 2+ SROs / 2 SRO-FTs, and all 23 tracked official accounts remain linked from their SROs' own sites (checked 16 November). [14] [15]

The full paper trail is in our exit story note. [9] [10] [11]

## The SROs at work

**IRDAI: the count is now two lakh, and the calendar has one date on it.** The week's reporting put scale and sequence on the distribution reform fight. ET Now, 13 November: more than 2,00,000 comments and suggestions received from around 15,000 stakeholders through a 32-section feedback form; the regulator is evaluating feedback and may modify proposals; draft regulations likely by mid-December 2026; final reforms expected to take effect from 1 April 2027. [1] CNBC-TV18, same day: IRDAI considering modifications to the draft distribution paper; a new draft likely by mid-December based on the feedback received. [4]

Do the arithmetic on the response count: two lakh comments from roughly 15,000 stakeholders is about thirteen comments per submitting entity — which is what organised campaign submissions look like on both sides of any contested consultation. Neither number tells you the quality of any single argument; the draft text in mid-December is where positions actually land. [1]

The lobbying map filled in too. The broker delegation that met the finance minister, the PMO and DFS on 10 November was told to take its objections to IRDAI chairman Ajay Seth — the government declining to overrule its own regulator's consultation mid-process. [3] IBAI's stated position: oppose hard caps, keep the 2023 EoM framework until its scheduled 2028 review, commission an independent impact assessment, and warnings about livelihoods and competition. [3] Add what we know from last fortnight — FIDC asking for a five-year glide path on credit-linked commissions from 45–49% down to 25%, banks pricing their bancassurance losses, LIC saying it is already inside the proposed lines [2] — and the shape of the fight is consistent: every distributor wants transition time, and nobody is yet arguing about what the borrower gets.

That last question is the one this register will keep asking. The consultation's stated purpose includes addressing "excessive commissions and mis-selling" and improving policyholder outcomes. [3] The test when the mid-December draft lands: does the compressed commission show up in the borrower's premium or loan pricing, or does it merely re-balance margins inside the distribution chain?

**Sahamati: the next-chapter speech is on the record, and one of its dates isn't.** SamvAAd 2026 — held 14–15 October at Grand Hyatt Mumbai, the SRO's first flagship summit since its RBI recognition for the AA ecosystem — produced a published record this week: the CEO welcome address went up as a blog on 12 November and as video on 11 November. [5] [6] The numbers, per the address: 500 million+ consents fulfilled, 350 million accounts linked, nearly 1,200 entities live on the network as of September 2026; AA-enabled lending at approximately ₹35,000 crore in monthly disbursements in H2 FY26, with a fivefold growth ambition over two years. [5] [6] The seven priorities include interoperability (following RBI's AA interoperability announcement and subsequent technical specifications), a Unified Dashboard of Consumer Consents proof-of-concept, and — first on the list — "building a sustainable economic model." [5] Chairman R. Gandhi's own address framed the transition as moving "from building the network to building the institutions that sustain it." [17]

Now the date problem. Priority one, as published on 12 November: "The AA ecosystem's economic model, effective from 1 November 2026..." [5] That is eleven days after Sahamati's own 26 October circular deferred the pricing switch-on to 1 December, citing the festival season and requests for postponement. [7] Read charitably: the blog summarises a speech delivered 15 October, before the deferral, and nobody edited the tense. Read less charitably: the SRO's flagship publication, out this week, still carries the old start date while the fee amounts themselves have never appeared in any public document. Either way, the operational fact stands: the meter starts 1 December, and as of today there is still no public fee card to read. [5] [7]

**UFF: a merger on paper, two member counts, one missing row.** The Q2 FY27 newsletter — Volume 14, covering July–September 2026 — is the fullest written account yet of the India Fintech Foundation merger. Its record: the 8th India Fintech Conclave (12 August 2026, Taj Lands End, Mumbai) was the first edition since "the merger of the Unified Fintech Forum and the India Fintech Foundation into a single industry body, now uniting 200+ member institutions under one voice"; the 46th UFF board meeting (23 September 2026) was convened "to finalise on Merger Options of UFF-IFF"; and post-recognition, UFF met RBI's FinTech Department — CGM Suvendu Pati and senior officials — on 15 September 2026 to discuss its SRO-FT role. [8] UFF's conclave site advertises the same "200+ member institutions" and IFF's own site now operates "under the legal entity name of Unified Fintech Forum." [18] [19]

Against that, the verifiable counts on UFF's own properties: the About page says "At present UFF has 120 members" (re-fetched today), and the member wall lists 120 organisations with BSFG Finance missing since 1 October — no public statement from either party, now day 46. [9] [10] [11] The 200+ is a marketing claim about a combined body whose member-level accounting we cannot yet see; the 120 is a number UFF publishes beside a wall that doesn't add up to its own About text. Both come from UFF. The register's position is unchanged: a wall without dates and a count without a list are claims, not records — and when one body publishes two different member counts in the same month, the only cure is a dated register with additions and exits.

**FACE: the collection norms show up in the field.** Business Standard's 15 November feature on how technology, data and consent norms are reshaping debt collection credits the rollout of FACE's debt-recovery guidelines — released September 2025, covering governance, agent conduct, borrower disclosure, data protection and AI use across digital and field recoveries — as part of what is changing, and quotes CEO Sugandh Saxena: "Regulations clearly define no-go zones for collections protecting customers' rights to privacy and fair treatment." [12] This is the SRO's standards work being cited as market-shaping rather than shelfware — worth noting, and equally worth holding to the same standard: guidelines shape outcomes only if enforced, and FACE's enforcement record is not yet publicly measurable. [12]

An ETBFSI explainer (10 November) used the two-SRO-FT framework as its subject: what powers RBI gave FACE and UFF, where their authority ends, how they differ, and the challenges facing the setup. [13] The short version for consumers: these bodies monitor compliance, set codes and escalate to RBI — they cannot fine, license or refund you. A complaint about a specific lender still goes to the lender's grievance desk, then RBI Ombudsman; the SRO is a lever, not a court. [13]

## What it means for consumers

- **Mid-December is the date that matters for insurance distribution.** The draft regulations are expected around mid-December, with effect from 1 April 2027 the current working assumption. [1] When the draft lands, the only number that matters is the one attached to credit-life and bundled products — the segment where commissions run 45–49% today. If the final rules do not require the commission saving to reach your premium or loan pricing, the reform has re-balanced industry margins and called it consumer protection. [2] [3]
- **The 2 lakh comments are the industry's noise, not your voice.** Thirteen comments per submitting entity, on average, is what organised campaigns produce. [1] If you want an individual position on record, IBAI's demand for an independent impact assessment is a template — but the leverage moment for consumers is the draft review window, not the done deal.
- **1 December: the AA meter starts with no published prices.** The one consumer guarantee remains: you cannot be charged for sharing your own data through the network. If any lender adds a "statement fee" or "processing fee" after 1 December, ask whether it is the AA pass-through in disguise. And note that the SRO's own flagship publication this week still described the model as starting 1 November — if an institution quotes you a date, ask which document it read. [5] [7]
- **Two member counts from one SRO is your cue to distrust roster claims.** UFF says 200+ institutions in its newsletter and 120 members on its About page in the same month. [8] [9] Until SROs publish dated membership registers — additions, exits, reasons — every roster number anywhere is a floor, and the captures we keep are the only public record of the movement.
- **Grievance month is still running.** November remains IRDAI's Grievance Resolution Month; if your insurance complaint is among the 72% stuck over a year, this is the month to escalate via the Bima Bharosa portal. [2]
