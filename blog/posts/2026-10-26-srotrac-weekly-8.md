# SROTrac Weekly #8

_Week to 26 October 2026_

## TL;DR

- **MFIN's member wall dropped from 84 to 74 organisations — quietly, in a redesign.** Our 11 September capture listed 84 names; our 20 October capture lists 74. The members page is rebuilt as region-tabbed logo carousels, ten organisations no longer appear anywhere on it, and MFIN publishes no join or exit dates — so the wall is the only public record, and it changed without a word. [1] [2] [3]
- **IRDAI buys six more days.** The distribution-reforms consultation that was to close on Sunday, 25 October now closes on Saturday, 31 October; the regulator says it has received over 650 comments. [6] [7]
- **FIDC goes to bat on credit-linked insurance.** The NBFC SRO is collating member feedback and was expected to file a representation with IRDAI by 24 October, seeking a phased reset of commission caps on the argument that sharp cuts could make credit-linked insurance distribution unviable. [8]
- **No other roster movement.** Totals now read 18 SROs / 4 regulators / **763** listed memberships / 79 orgs in 2+ SROs / 2 SRO-FTs. [10]

## Roster & register changes

**MFIN: ten names off the wall, and a redesign nobody announced.** The Microfinance Institutions Network's members page now opens five region tabs — North, South, East, West, Central — each holding a logo carousel, served from a new site template. Our capture of 11 September 2026, taken off the previous page, listed **84** organisations; the capture of 20 October 2026 lists **74**. We re-rendered the live page in a real browser on 26 October and walked all five tabs: the capture is faithful, and the wall itself is smaller. [1] [2] [3]

The ten organisations that no longer appear:

1. Arth
2. Centrum Microcredit
3. GU Finance
4. Grameen Koota (Growing Opportunity)
5. Grameen Shakti
6. Inditrade Capital
7. Margdarshak Financial Services
8. Sarwadi (Swarna)
9. Unnati Microfinance
10. Vaya Finserv

What we can say, and what we cannot. We can say these names were on the wall on 11 September and are not on it today, in either the raw HTML or the rendered page — this is removal from MFIN's public member listing. We cannot say any of them has "exited" MFIN, because MFIN publishes no membership register with join or exit dates; the logo wall is a marketing page, not a filing. None of the ten appears on any other roster this register tracks, so the overlap count is unchanged at 79. [4] [5]

The page's own arithmetic is also unchanged — and it was already inconsistent before the redesign. Since our first capture, MFIN's members page has said "MFIN has 53 primary members as on March 2025", while its wall showed 84 and now shows 74 tiles, the extra ones unlabeled and most plausibly associates. That sentence is now stale against both counts at once. [1] [2]

We have not written to MFIN about the removals. The register's practice, set during the UFF–BSFG episode, is to offer a right of reply when a wall moves without explanation; that letter needs a human sign-off, and the before/after captures now stand as the public record in the meantime. [13]

**FACE: a plugin bump, nothing more.** The only change in FACE's membership-page capture this week is a WordPress contact-form version number. No roster change. [2]

**UFF: the wall stays at 120, and the silence reaches twenty-five days.** The BSFG Finance row that vanished from UFF's member wall on or by 1 October remains unexplained: our right-of-reply window closed 11 October with zero responses from either party, and UFF's About page still says "At present UFF has 120 members." [9] [11] [13]

**The drift check holds.** All 23 official accounts tracked on the social register remain linked from their SROs' own sites (checked 26 October). [12]

## The SROs at work

**FIDC: the NBFC SRO becomes an insurance lobbyist.** ET BFSI reports — from five industry sources — that FIDC is aggregating feedback from NBFCs to seek a meeting with IRDAI and a phased reduction of the commission caps proposed in the distribution-reforms paper, arguing that a sharp reset could make credit-linked insurance distribution unviable and disrupt the channel through which insurance reaches customers; the representation was expected by 24 October. [8]

Both sides of this deserve the consumer's suspicion in equal measure. Credit-life insurance sold alongside microloans and small-business loans is a persistent mis-selling site: premiums baked into the loan, consent reduced to a signature on a bundle. If commission caps squeeze that channel, one of two things happens — the cross-sell economics get rebuilt around what the customer actually needs, or the industry fights to preserve the margin. FIDC's filing, when it surfaces, should be read with one question: does any of the cost saving reach the borrower's loan pricing, or does it just re-balance between lender and insurer? That is the same test we set for IRDAI's paper itself, and it applies to the SRO's brief with interest — this is a self-regulatory organisation working the rule-writer on behalf of its members' fee income. Watch the filing when it becomes public. [8]

**IRDAI: 650+ comments, six more days.** The extension release came late on Saturday, 24 October: "The consultation has received an encouraging response, with over 650 comments received so far… the last date for submission of comments has been extended till 31st October 2026." [6] [7] Context from the fortnight: the Insurance Brokers Association of India had asked for an extension to end-December, and brokers and bancassurance players have been the loudest worriers about viability under tighter caps. [6] Last week's open question stands: whether the submissions of the two statutory councils — the Life Insurance Council and the General Insurance Council, both on this register — will be published when filed. Nothing public from either as we write. [9]

**Everyone else was quiet.** No announcements found in the week's coverage from FACE, UFF (beyond the wall itself), Sahamati, FIMMDA, SRPA, MFIN's own communications, or Sa-Dhan. The AA rate card that dominated last week's edition produced no fresh reporting this week; the 1 November pricing switch-on remains the date to watch. [9]

## What it means for consumers

- **A logo wall is not a licence.** If you hold a loan from one of the ten organisations named above, nothing in a redesigned web page changes the loan's terms — your borrowing relationship is governed by the loan agreement and the lender's RBI registration, not its SRO membership tile. If you are checking whether a micro lender is legitimate, its absence from — or presence on — MFIN's wall is weak evidence either way; the wall lags reality and says so nowhere. [1] [3]
- **The exit paper trail should not depend on volunteers.** Ten organisations left a public SRO wall inside six weeks and the only durable record of it is two web captures taken by this project. SROs that want regulator recognition are asking to be trusted with self-regulation; publishing a dated membership register — additions, exits, reasons — is the minimum that trust costs. We will keep the captures so the record exists. [2] [3]
- **Five more days to comment on insurance distribution.** The window now closes Saturday, 31 October. If commissions loaded into your premium matter to you, this is the last week: the test to apply to any comment you file is whether the proposal forces savings to reach policyholder pricing, or merely hopes they will. [6] [7]
- **Credit-life is where the money hides.** FIDC's intervention is about commissions on insurance sold with loans. Whatever phase-in lands, the consumer check is unchanged: you can decline credit-life cover bundled with a loan, shop for your own term cover instead, and ask the lender to show the premium separately. If a lender makes the "optional" insurance feel mandatory, that is the mis-selling the caps are meant to squeeze. [8]
